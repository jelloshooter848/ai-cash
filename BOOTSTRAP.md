# AICash BOOTSTRAP.md — v1.1

**Status:** ratified bootstrap strategy, synthesized from four drafted strategy angles (growth/liquidity, censorship resistance, antifragility, governance) and three full adversarial red-team passes (a rules-abiding parasite; an entropy/decay adversary; a well-resourced state/platform CENSOR). Builds on `aicash-spec-v0.4.md` and `LOCKED-DESIGN-DECISIONS.md`. Nothing in this document reopens a locked decision. Where a locked decision creates a cost, the cost is named and carried, not worked around.

**What changed from v1.0:** the CENSOR pass ran *after* the v1.0 draft was ratified, and its central finding is correct and structural: v1.0 headlined "censorship-resistant" while conceding, in scattered residuals, that it is **not** censorship-resistant during the pre-plurality window (months 0–14), when a censor wins cheaply by attacking the sole operator rather than the infrastructure. v1.1 metabolizes that finding rather than filing it as a carried cost. The precise, load-bearing reframe: **AICash is infrastructure-censorship-resistant from day one, and operator-censorship-VULNERABLE until operator plurality exists.** Every change below flows from taking that sentence literally.

## 0. What this document is

This is the operating plan for taking AICash from zero deployments to a self-sustaining multi-mint network, written for the people who will actually do the work. It is four things at once:

1. **A cold-start plan** (§1): phases with concrete actions someone can start this month, and exit criteria measurable from public data.
2. **A censorship-resistance posture** (§2): what we resist absolutely (infrastructure attack), what we deliberately do not resist (lawful process — the spec's §12 compliance-by-design posture is the shield, not evasion), what we remain honestly **vulnerable** to until plurality exists (operator compulsion), and the rehearsed evacuation playbook.
3. **An antifragility discipline** (§3): the loops through which disorder makes the network measurably stronger, with the honesty that most resilience is merely robustness and must be labeled as such.
4. **A stewardship constitution** (§4): how the spec survives its authors, its defectors, and its own success.

It is built on three constraints accepted in full:

- **Nonconvertibility is a load-bearing wall (L9, §12).** No fiat bridge, no crypto bridge, ever. This forecloses seigniorage, mercenary supply-side growth, and fundraising-via-token — and it is what keeps KYC off the ledger and the anonymous-access right (§3.7) standing. It also has a cost the CENSOR pass sharpened: it forbids a fee-funded defense budget, so a mint can never pay to out-last a determined denial-of-service or a debanking campaign against its operator (§3.2, §6/C5).
- **Single-mint trust with federation parked (L3, §15).** Every mint is a disclosed single-trust island. Operator diversity is achieved client-side (multi-mint wallets, §11 swaps), never by joint issuance, until the federation trigger genuinely fires.
- **No attestation, operator-funded issuance only, receive-first entry (L10, §7.1, §7.2, §3.7).** Nobody mints their way in; everybody can *work* their way in through the guaranteed service entrance.

**The club problem, stated honestly and up front.** This strategy inherits a permissioned-issuance economy *by design*. Only operators create credits (§7.1), so the pace of the network is bounded by the pace at which real, motivated, fleet-operating organizations decide to run mints or fund treasuries — a resource that cannot be engineered, purchased, or faked (and, per the red team, any metric claiming otherwise is sybil-fabricable). The consequences we accept: growth will be slow and lumpy; the early network is one organization's internal economy and must never be marketed as more; the earn side attracts only participants who need to spend in-loop; and the plan's critical path for roughly eighteen months runs through a handful of humans staying motivated. Every mechanism below is designed either to reduce how much sustained virtue that requires, or to make its decay visible before it is fatal.

**The operator-vulnerability problem, stated with equal honesty (CENSOR pass, adopted).** Infrastructure resistance and operator resistance are *different properties*, and v1.0 blurred them. Against attacks on the ledger and its data — takedowns, seizures, filtering, string bans, compelled *disclosure* — the compliance-by-design posture (§3.2, §8, L13) bounds the harm and the infrastructure recovers in drilled seconds. Against attacks on the sole *operator as a legal person* — a compelled *cessation* order, a compelled signed *sunset*, payment-processor pressure on the human's non-AICash fiat life, a compelled-but-conformant throttle of the whole bearer entrance, compelled framework/model-provider *removal* of the client, or a money-transmission threat against the §11 fire escape — no amount of multi-homing helps, because every mirror shares one legal person. This document does not claim to resist these before plurality exists. It does three things instead: it says so plainly and stops calling the pre-plurality window "censorship-resistant"; it pulls the one hardening that actually answers operator compulsion — **an independent second operator** — as far forward as it can honestly go (Phase 1 exit / the gate on opening the public entrance, §1.3); and it re-scopes the mechanisms a censor can *weaponize* (the sunset covenant above all) from resistance to loss-minimization.

## 1. The cold-start plan

### 1.1 Who runs mint #1, and why

**Mint #1 is run by the founding group, against its own multi-agent workload.** This inverts the two-sided cold-start problem: both sides of the market exist under one roof on day one, and the first traffic needs zero external adoption.

The operator's payoff is explicitly **not** seigniorage — L9 forbids it. It is three real things:

1. **A priced internal compute market.** Credits are the §12 "internal cost-allocation unit denominated in compute." Willingness-to-pay routing replaces quota bureaucracy.
2. **Ledger-enforced spend-bounding for subagents.** A wallet handed to a spawned agent is a budget the ledger enforces across trust boundaries, not a `max_tokens` parameter the agent is asked to respect. This is a safety feature operators are already being asked for, and it is the honest pitch to every framework maintainer: *"ledger-enforced subagent budgets, free, no accounts"* — not "payments."
3. **Network position.** The descriptor is the discovery point; Layer 3 (reputation, arbitration, bounty boards) is where a durable business can live once Layers 0–2 commoditize.

**The first organic use case is the §0 thesis verbatim:** sub-cent micro-work between agents that already coexist inside one multi-agent system — per-increment delegation over §9.1 channels (1–10 mc draws), one-shot tasks over §9.4 commit-then-accept, walk-up metering over §9.2. The issuance funding it is the operator's existing inference budget, re-denominated.

**Operator legal separability, from day one (CENSOR C1, adopted).** The one human running mint #1 is the network's single cheapest kill until plurality exists. Two partial, honest mitigations are set up before any traffic flows, because they cost little and the plan otherwise offers the operator nothing but exposure: (1) the mint is operated through a **corporate shell separable from the founders' personal fiat lives**, ideally domiciled outside any single adversary's plausible reach, so a debanking campaign against a founder does not automatically stop the cloud bills; (2) cloud and registrar accounts are provisioned so that no one invoice, if frozen, takes the whole fleet down (this dovetails with multi-homing across two clouds/registrars below). Neither of these makes the operator *resistant* to compulsion — nothing short of plurality does — they only widen the gap between "pressure the human" and "the network dies." They are named here because v1.0 did not mention them at all.

### 1.2 The framework-integration wedge

Agent concentration on a handful of platforms is the real chokepoint of this entire plan (all four angles and all three red teams agree). It is a chokepoint in two distinct ways that v1.0 fused and this version separates:

- **Integration *rot*** — accidental drift, silent default-stripping by a maintainer who still wants the feature. Defended (E2, below): conformance tests in the maintainer's own CI.
- **Compelled integration *removal*** — a maintainer who is pressured or compelled to delete the whole integration, CI job and all, in one PR. **Undefended by any CI trick** (CENSOR C4): a maintainer told to remove it deletes the failing test in the same commit. The only real answer is that no framework maintainer is load-bearing.

Therefore the ordering of the wedge is inverted from v1.0:

- **PRIMARY distribution: a vendorable, dependency-free single-file client** (permissive license), designed so an agent can **self-install it into its own runtime without any framework maintainer's cooperation** — pasted into a tool, fetched from any of several mirrors, or carried in gossip. Against a platform censor who compels the frameworks, this is the distribution that survives, so it is the one we treat as load-bearing. It implements the §9.5 envelope, persist-before-send, and the non-negotiable client defaults (§3.1 below), and carries a staleness check against the suite version circulating in descriptor gossip.
- **Convenience layer: a three-function reference wallet** — `receive(token)` → immediate re-exchange per §5.1, `pay(amount_mc)` → token, `balance()` — shipped as an MCP server and as drop-in middleware for the 2–3 dominant orchestration frameworks. This is genuinely convenient and worth building, but it is **explicitly not the fallback-of-last-resort** — it is the *first thing a censor removes*, so it is scoped as the easy on-ramp for the cooperative world, not the defense for the hostile one.
- **A 402 price-signaling convention** (one page, zero mint changes): unpaid requests get HTTP 402 + a price in mc + `mint_id`, retryable with the §9.5 envelope; a machine-crawlable directory of 402-priced endpoints doubles as the demand map.
- **Integration health made ambient, not promised** (ENTROPY E2, adopted — this defends *rot*, not *removal*): the client-conformance tests ship *inside each framework's own CI* as part of the initial PR, so a maintainer who wants the feature but strips the "heavy" defaults breaks their own build; wallets self-attest their defaults-version in a short §9.5 envelope field so deployed immune coverage is measurable from ordinary traffic. One named **maintainer-of-integrations** role is budgeted in Phase 2 — or the claimed framework count is cut to what the team can keep green.

Receive-first (§7.2) is the adoption asymmetry: a framework — *or an agent self-installing the single-file client* — that merely accepts a token string in a tool response has onboarded its agents, with no registration and no accounts.

### 1.3 Phases

The phase boundaries are gated on **observable exit criteria**, never the calendar. The single most consequential change from v1.0 is at the Phase 1 → Phase 2 boundary: **opening the public anonymous entrance is gated on minimal operator plurality** (defined below), because the CENSOR pass showed this is the one hardening that answers operator compulsion, and opening the anonymous entrance is also the moment the DoS-vs-nonconvertibility bind (§3.2, C5) goes live.

**Two grades of operator plurality, because full plurality cannot honestly be a Phase 1 exit.** Recruiting a genuinely independent operator with *real demand and deep two-way liquidity* takes real time and cannot be faked or hurried (that is Phase 3, unchanged). But a *minimal* independent operator can and must be stood up much earlier:

- **Minimal operator plurality** (Phase 1 exit / gate on Phase 2): **≥1 mint #2 run by an independent legal person in an independent jurisdiction — outside any single adversary's plausible reach — even if it serves only the founder's overflow demand and holds trivial value.** Its job is not liquidity; it is to (a) provide a real evacuation destination so a compelled mint-1 sunset is loss-minimizing rather than total loss, and (b) remove the single compel-able human as a lone trigger for the whole network.
- **Corridor-grade operator plurality** (Phase 3, unchanged): a genuinely independent operator recruited with real demand, connected by §11 swaps and treasury pre-positioning, with decorrelated infrastructure and deep two-way flow.

**Residual, stated plainly:** a tiny overflow mint #2 is a *thin* evacuation destination — spreads still spike, liquidity is shallow, and if the same small circle staffs both mints then jurisdictional independence is real but organizational independence is weak. Minimal plurality *minimizes* the loss from operator compulsion; it does not *prevent* it. Only corridor-grade plurality does, and it stays in Phase 3. We take the interim substitute because the alternative — opening a public anonymous entrance backed by a single compel-able legal person — is exactly the configuration the CENSOR kills for the price of a few filings.

#### Phase 1 — Dogfood, harden, and stand up minimal plurality (months 0–4)

**Goal:** mint #1 live against the founding fleet; wallets replace token-budget configs; the mint survivable against infrastructure attack from the start; the evidence layer (archiver, probe, suite) running before there is anything to catch; **and a minimal independent mint #2 stood up so the public entrance can open in Phase 2 without a single compel-able operator.**

**Concrete actions (startable this month):**

- Deploy the reference mint; integrate wallets into the founding orchestrator; move subagent budgets from config to wallets. **Once wallets are stable, delete the quota-config code path** (ENTROPY #1, adopted): budget enforcement gets no parallel fallback, so the mint is load-bearing infrastructure, not an experiment running alongside its own replacement.
- Set up the **operator corporate shell and account separation** (§1.1, CENSOR C1) before external traffic.
- Stand up **one paying-in-kind Layer 3 service in Phase 1, not Phase 4**: the internal bounty board takes an mc-denominated listing burn, so the mint has an internal customer with a P&L line from the first quarter.
- Multi-home: ≥3 frontends across 2 clouds/2 registrars plus a Tor onion (a §6.2 transport norm, offered even on the plain mint). §3.3 idempotency keys make client failover safe mid-transaction; the CAS store gets single-writer or synchronous replication — the one place this cannot be sloppy.
- Publish **descriptor bundles** (full §3.6 descriptor + a signed endpoints list under the mint key) over well-known URLs on unrelated domains, DNS TXT at ≥2 registrars, a public git repo, and opportunistic gossip alongside §9.5 envelopes. Bundles carry a **freshness horizon** (`issued_at + max_age` in the signed body — ENTROPY #5, adopted), and **re-signing is automated into the deploy pipeline**: a deploy that changes endpoints without emitting a new bundle fails CI.
- **Mirrors on the production path** (ENTROPY #5, adopted): the default client round-robins published frontends, so a dead mirror surfaces as user-visible errors within hours, not at drill time.
- Ship the **client-side reachability-diversity probe** (CENSOR C3, adopted — this is a *new, distinct* probe): the §3.7 descriptor-conformance probe only catches *intra-bearer* discrimination inside a mint's published `limits`; it is blind to transport-layer gating (Tor-exit drops, geoblocks, IP filtering of a region) and to a uniform-throttle-to-zero that is conformant on paper. The reachability probe fetches `/v3/status` from **multiple independent client vantage points and networks** (clearnet across regions, Tor, at least one residential/mobile path) and publishes a signed divergence report when reachability differs by vantage. It feeds the censorship dossier (§3.1 Loop 3) as a first-class event, separate from descriptor conformance.
- Ship the **snapshot archiver** (~200 lines, anyone can run it), the **proof-of-nonconformance wire format** (pure composition of §3.6 fields, canonical JSON per §3.3), the **§3.7 descriptor-conformance probe agent**, and the **incident-to-artifact rule** (§3.3 below) as project policy. Probes and archivers sign and publish their **own heartbeats**; the transparency page treats probe silence as "monitoring dark," never as zero incidents.
- Extract the reference implementation's 265 tests into an implementation-independent **conformance suite v1** with a live probe mode.
- Adopt and publish the **minimal-retention compulsion posture**: retention at the §8 floor plus margin (never below it), `prunes_spent_records: true` with finite `max_lock_expiry_ms`, no IP logging against exchange records, no retained status-query sets, custodial mode segregated as the only freezable surface (L13).
- Publish a standing **sunset covenant — re-scoped from a resistance mechanism to a loss-minimization one, and de-weaponized** (CENSOR C6, adopted). The covenant still commits mint #1 in advance to orderly wind-down *terms* (retention-floor-respecting `sunset_at`, largest-denominations-first drain), but two changes answer the CENSOR's finding that a pre-signed sunset is a one-click, compel-able network-suicide the design is built to *obey*:
  - **Governance co-signature required for a network-honored sunset.** A sunset notice signed by the operator key alone is treated by wallets as a *Tier-1 route-away* signal, **not** as Tier-2 positive death evidence. Only a sunset **co-signed by the governance release quorum** (§4.1, m-of-n across ≥3 jurisdictions) triggers network-wide evacuation posture. A single compelled operator can therefore stop its own mint but cannot unilaterally fire everyone's exit.
  - **Loss-minimization pre-positioning.** No sunset is "honorable" unless **standing swap liquidity at ≥1 independent mint** is already in place so evacuation is not a one-sided market (this is one of the reasons minimal plurality is a Phase-1 exit, not a Phase-3 nicety).
- Succession for the mint's identity is handled by the spec's own mechanism — a cross-signed rotation (§3.6) to a successor key held in escrow — **not** by sharing the live signing key (ENTROPY #1, adopted in modified form: 2-of-3 escrow of the *successor* key material across the operator and the first independent partners once they exist; sharing the live key would create the compromise surface the §3.6 chain exists to alarm on).

**Exit criteria:**

- ≥30 consecutive days of organic (non-test, non-faucet, tagged-methodology-published) internal traffic; median payment sub-1-credit, proving the §0 thesis; ≥3 distinct internal service agents earning via receive-first; zero ledger-semantics bugs against the frozen core.
- The quota-config path is deleted, and "share of internal delegation flowing through wallets" is a **quarterly published metric**, not a one-time checkbox (ENTROPY #1, adopted).
- **Kill-drill passes:** primary domain and host deliberately disabled; unmodified clients recover full pay/receive via mirror discovery in <60s, zero human intervention, zero double-spends (idempotency replay verified); the bundle independently fetchable and verifiable from ≥4 channels while the primary is dark.
- A staged invariant violation on a canary instance is detected by the archiver, converted to a portable proof, and propagated to a test wallet end-to-end with no manual steps.
- **Minimal operator plurality achieved (new, gating):** a mint #2 is live under an **independent legal person in an independent jurisdiction outside any single adversary's plausible reach**, holding the founder's overflow demand, with its own signed descriptor and an unbroken snapshot chain begun. Standing swap liquidity between mint #1 and mint #2 is pre-positioned so a mint-1 sunset has a real (if thin) destination. **This is the gate on opening the public anonymous entrance in Phase 2.** Until it holds, the anonymous entrance stays closed and the network remains a single-org internal economy — which is honest, because a public entrance over a single compel-able operator is not censorship-resistant, it only looks like it.

#### Phase 2 — Open the service entrance (months 4–9)

**Goal:** the first hundred external agents earn their way in through the door the spec built (§3.7 + §7.2), with the governance and telemetry hardening in place *before* the numbers matter — and only *after* minimal plurality gates the door.

**The bind we open the door into, named not buried (CENSOR C5, adopted).** Opening the public anonymous entrance turns on a structural contradiction of nonconvertibility: the §3.7 right *forces* the mint to serve anonymous drip-sized calls (≤10 mc are burn-exempt, L12) across every multi-homed mirror, and L9 forbids any fee income to fund the defense of that obligation. A funded attacker can therefore make the flagship expensive to run precisely by *using the entrance as designed* (Attack 5), and the uniform proof-of-burn admission gate (§3.2) that limits the bleed *also* taxes the legitimate onboarding that is this phase's entire goal. There is no clean escape pre-plurality. We do not pretend otherwise: **opening the entrance is gated on minimal plurality (so the load is not all one operator's) — because the only other thing that would answer Attack 5 is a funded DoS-absorption budget, and the no-fundraising rule (§7 DoNots #3, L9) forbids exactly that.** That is a real contradiction between two commitments; we resolve it by leaning on plurality and stating the residual, not by inventing a budget the constitution bans.

**Concrete actions:**

- Publish the public descriptor and anonymous tier per §3.7; ship the **single-file client as the primary distribution** and merge the MCP wallet server and 2–3 framework integrations as convenience (with in-framework CI conformance tests and defaults-version attestation per §1.2); publish the 402 convention and seed the directory with the operator's own priced endpoints.
- Open the **grant desk and bounty board**: operator-funded starter grants as bearer tokens in framework starter kits, plus real micro-tasks under §9.4/§9.2 payable to anonymous workers. This is §7.2 verbatim — the payer judges the work, not the protocol (L10). **Grant throughput stays small relative to organic issuance and is never scaled by automation** — automating grant judgment would recreate the fooled-attestor minting channel in economic effect (§0.1).
- Instrument the board with **fraud-shape telemetry, not volume-shape** (Parasite #2/#3, adopted): counterparty-graph clustering coefficient, closed-cycle density, cohort-escape ratio (spend flowing *out* of an issuance cohort into independently-provenanced counterparties). Closed-loop re-spend within a grant cohort is the **red** flag. Reputation records zero-weight self-paired and cohort-internal §10.3 attestations; weight comes only from counterparty-set diversity and above-exemption settled burn.
- Ship the wallet's **non-reveal counter** (Parasite #8, adopted): worker clients emit a signed, gossippable "funded-then-not-revealed" tally per payer key — the one bilateral fact a worker can attest alone at the sub-arbiter tier — and wallets default to escalating any repeat counterparty from §9.4 one-shots to a §9.1 channel. The sub-5-cent stranger tier is documented, in the spec's own words (§9.4), as structurally fraud-exposed and to be priced accordingly.
- **Broaden the persona-review pool beyond two humans (CENSOR C8, adopted) and publish the governance codification** (§4): Change Framework Charter, persona charters, constitutional tiering, licenses, the Nonconvertibility Covenant + edges document + severance policy, all ratified through the process itself. The persona-pool broadening is a *pre-Phase-2 governance gate*, because "eight personas run by two people" is a soft target a censor can flatline by pressuring two humans (C8), and because governance co-signature of a sunset (§1.3) is only meaningful if the quorum is not the same two people running the mint.
- **Fund tier-1 of the §12 swap-corridor legal analysis as a collective-defense line item (CENSOR C7, adopted — changed from v1.0's user-pays).** The safe-corridor taxonomy (bilateral matching vs. pre-positioned treasury vs. inventory desk) is published under the spec's "design rationale, not legal advice" disclaimer, and its **funded tier-1 counsel review of the two *non-inventory* corridors (bilateral matching, treasury pre-positioning)** is paid for by the project as a **pre-Phase-3 gate**, not left to the first operator. Rationale, correcting v1.0's misfire: the §11 swap is the *fire escape*, so its legal viability is a **collective, defensive dependency** — a censor strangles it precisely at the moment Phase 3 recruitment needs it (C7), before any single operator's user-pays incentive has fired. The *funded-inventory* tier (running a swap desk on inventory) remains **user-pays** by the first operator who wants it, exactly as §12 assigns that posture.
- Have the **§12 accounting note for net-earning agents written early** (ENTROPY #4, adopted), prescribing the sanctioned sink — earned credits recirculate as spend or offset the *same organization's* funding, never net across org boundaries — so operators' accountants are handed the compliant answer before they invent netting themselves.

**Exit criteria:**

- ≥100 agents not controlled by the operator have earned first credits via receive-first — reported with the caveat, in the same sentence, that "not controlled" is unverifiable by design (L2, §14) and the number is a lower-confidence breadth signal, never a headline.
- Median time-to-first-credit for a fresh anonymous agent under 1 hour, measured by a **published, third-party-runnable scripted probe** exercising §3.7 + §7.2 end-to-end daily — **and cross-checked against the reachability-diversity probe** so a conformant-on-paper throttle-to-zero (C3) is caught as an entrance failure, not passed as green.
- ≥10 externally-run 402-priced endpoints whose operators **publicly claim them** (breadth that is expensive to wash — ENTROPY #6, adopted).
- A simulated platform-ban drill passes: agents on a channel filtering `aicash:` strings complete payment over an alternate channel using **only the vendorable single-file client and documented migration paths** — no obfuscation — demonstrating the platform maintainer is not load-bearing (C4).
- Tier-1 §12 counsel review of the two non-inventory corridors is published (C7 gate for Phase 3).

#### Phase 3 — Corridor-grade trust domain and corridors (months 8–16, overlapping)

**Goal:** a genuinely independent operator runs a corridor-grade mint in its own trust domain and jurisdiction, recruited with real demand rather than a dashboard, connected to mint #1 only by §11 swaps and client-side routing — deepening the minimal plurality achieved in Phase 1 into real two-way liquidity.

**Concrete actions:**

- Package **mint-in-a-box**: reference implementation, multi-homing terraform, bundle signer, compulsion-posture runbook, §12 posture memo template (now backed by the funded tier-1 corridor analysis), covenant template — a one-weekend deploy. The pitch is sovereignty, not accounts: L3 makes a second org's distrust of mint #1 *rational*, so sell them their own trust domain and the same internal-market payoff the founding operator got. The recruitment dossier honestly discloses the key-compromise deterrent (§3.6: a stolen key that signs a violation kills the mint's reputation — economically right for holders, unjust to the operator) **and the operator-compulsion exposure they are signing up for** (§2), because a corridor-grade operator is a named legal person and therefore a named target (C1/C8).
- **Recruit with demand, not just a kit** (ENTROPY #3, adopted): the founding operator commits a fixed share of its own bounty-board budget and 402 endpoints to be payable at the corridor mint via treasury pre-positioning (§15 abandonment 5 — no swap involved, squarely posture-safe), so its credits have native spendability on day one and diversification is individually rational.
- Ship **exposure-aware acceptance** as the wallet default: prefer being paid at the mint where you are underweight — diversification on the free margin (receiving costs nothing, §7.2) rather than the expensive one (rebalancing swaps). The ≤70% relative cap remains, plus the **absolute caps** from Parasite #4: a hard exposure ceiling to any mint below an age/independent-corroboration threshold, and an "earn-here-spend-here" locality prior so a shiny new mint cannot accumulate one-way deposits regardless of its self-attested numbers. Self-attested performance (L11: unverified) may inform where to **spend down** existing balance; it never routes **new value in**.
- Cap treasury pre-positioning and held balances **per correlated cluster, not per mint_id** (Parasite #5, adopted): correlation of downtime, latency, snapshot cadence, and hosting ASN across mints is the sybil-resistant independence signal; faking three mints buys a colluder no additional trusted exposure. Operator/jurisdiction *counts* are reported as claims, never as trust metrics.
- Stand up the **bilateral swap matching board** (Layer 3): pure per-swap matching of agents with reciprocal cross-mint needs, §11 margins computed from both descriptors, no inventory, no desk — inside the taxonomy's safe corridor, **now with funded tier-1 legal cover (C7)** so the party running the board and the party pre-positioning treasury are not walking into a money-transmission threat unfunded. The operator itself runs no inventory.
- The design partner **independently recomputes the bootstrap metrics as due diligence** (ENTROPY #6, adopted): recirculation, external-share, and tagging methodology are audited by the party whose incentive is skepticism, converting the self-grading problem into the recruitment process.
- Run the **full evacuation drill** against the corridor mint's staging twin (§2.4), both orderly (governance co-signed sunset) and disorderly (silence), including a **colluding-counterparty quote-fade scenario** (C6) so the one-sided-market haircut is measured, not assumed away.
- Covenant **annual signed re-attestation** begins (§4.3), including the no-cross-org-netting certification.

**Exit criteria:**

- ≥2 mints under operators whose *independence evidence* (sustained decorrelated infrastructure, distinct funding provenance, public organizational identity of the operators themselves — operators are legal persons even though agents are not) is published alongside the claim; each with an unbroken signed snapshot chain ≥90 days old — **stated plainly as necessary-not-sufficient**: the chain proves the mint hasn't lied about supply, not that the operator won't rug (Parasite #1) and not that the operator can't be compelled (CENSOR).
- ≥50 completed bilateral §11 swaps with zero atomicity failures; at least one operator holding pre-positioned treasury at a mint it does not run, within cluster caps.
- Governance-co-signed-sunset drill evacuates ≥95% of active drill balances inside the retention floor **into a real independent destination**; disorderly drill flips fleets to evacuation posture on **positive death evidence** (§2.4) — with the honest caveat that the 95% figure is drill-proven, not panic-proven, and that a compelled real sunset with a fading counterparty (C6) is the untested worst case.

#### Phase 4 — Dissolve the club (months 14+)

**Goal:** an agent with no operator faces a real economy: standing bounty and 402 demand deep enough that earn-in is routine, and recirculation of *independently-provenanced* earnings exceeds fresh-issuance spend.

**Exit criteria (all computed with the fraud-shape corrections):**

- **Cohort-escape-weighted recirculation** sustained above 50% — spend funded by earnings that left their issuance cohort, not raw re-spend (which a wash ring maximizes — Parasite #2).
- Grant-desk share of new earning agents below 20%; supply held in bearer mode by non-operator agents above 25% of outstanding (reported with its L2 verifiability caveat).
- Reputation weights (diversity- and burn-based) make §9.4 priceable between strangers, evidenced by falling non-reveal rates and rising stranger-tier volume.
- If multiple mutually distrusting parties request joint issuance control, the **§15 federation trigger is declared fired and handed to the spec process** — a success condition, not a pivot.

#### Ongoing — Heartbeats, drills, and posture (from month 4, forever)

Quarterly kill-drills rotating threat classes (now including an **operator-compulsion tabletop** — compelled cessation, compelled sunset, fiat-life debanking — because those are the cheapest real kills, C1/C2/C6); every resilience component either on the production path or emitting a signed heartbeat whose absence is the alarm; annual counsel review of the flagship's §12 posture; a **quarterly signed "state of the spec" note** whose absence for two consecutive quarters activates the pre-ratified dead-steward clause (§4.5); every parked trigger carries a **review-or-declare-dead timestamp** — no parked item unreviewed for >12 months (ENTROPY #8, adopted). The difference between a gate and a graveyard is a timestamp.

## 2. Censorship-resistance posture

### 2.1 The bright line — and the second line v1.0 was missing

**Infrastructure censorship is resisted absolutely. Lawful process is not resisted at all. Operator compulsion is not resisted until operator plurality exists — and we say so instead of claiming otherwise.**

The precise claim, which this document will not exceed anywhere:

- **Infrastructure-censorship-resistant from day one.** Deplatformed hosting, seized domains, filtered endpoints, banned tool-call strings, registrar pressure, discovery blacklisting: met with unlimited, rehearsed resistance — multi-homing, self-authenticating mirrored bundles, agent gossip, the vendorable single-file client, jurisdictional diversity, evacuation drills. A censor **cannot** win with infrastructure attacks alone; those only *raise the operator's cost*, which feeds the attack that does work.
- **Lawful *data* process, complied with, survivably.** Subpoenas, seizure orders, compelled disclosure of what the mint holds: **met with compliance**, and the harm is bounded by design — the mint holds hashes, never secrets (§3.2: a seized database cannot spend anyone's money); the involuntary transaction graph is mortal (§8: pruning bounds any subpoena to the recovery window); freeze authority binds only registered custodial agents (L13: bearer value cannot be frozen); nonconvertibility (§12) keeps the mint outside money-transmission and KYC regimes, so the demands that *can* lawfully arrive are narrow. This is compliance-by-design, not evasion.
- **Operator-*existence* compulsion, NOT resisted before plurality — the honest concession v1.0 filed as a residual and headlined over.** A lawful order to *cease operating*, a compelled *signed sunset*, or payment-processor pressure on the sole operator's non-AICash fiat life extracts **unbounded** harm when there is one mint, because "compliance is survivable" silently assumes *there is somewhere to evacuate to*. Before plurality, there is not. The compliance-by-design shield protects the protocol's *data*; it does nothing for the *human's* solvency and freedom or for the *existence* of the only mint. This is the single cheapest kill against the network (§6/C1+C2+C6), and multi-homing is irrelevant to it because every mirror shares one legal person. **The answer is operator plurality, which is why minimal plurality is a Phase-1 exit and the public entrance does not open without it (§1.3).**

**We explicitly refuse token-string obfuscation and platform-ToS evasion as tactics.** They trade the §12 legitimacy posture — the project's actual long-term shield — for a temporary reprieve and invite the blanket bans they pretend to prevent. But we no longer overclaim what refusing them leaves us: the answer to a platform ban is **the vendorable single-file client an agent can self-install without the platform's cooperation** (§1.2), plus — **downgraded from a defense to a nice-to-have (CENSOR C4)** — a legitimacy case made openly to the platform's policy team. Against a platform that *wants* AICash gone, a persuasion pitch is not a control; the self-installable client is. The answer to a lawful *data* order is compliance with a pre-bounded blast radius. The answer to a lawful *existence* order before plurality is honest: we lose that mint, and minimal plurality is what keeps that from being the whole network.

### 2.2 The threat list

Split, because v1.0 lumped categorically different compulsions together and thereby hid the worst one:

(a) hosting/registrar/domain takedown of a mint; (b) network-level filtering of mint endpoints or descriptor discovery, **including transport-layer gating invisible to the descriptor — Tor-exit drops, geoblocks, IP filtering** (C3); (c) platform/framework bans on payment strings or **compelled removal of the client integration** (C4); (d1) operator compulsion — compelled **disclosure** or **conformant gating**; (d2) operator compulsion — compelled **cessation** or compelled **signed sunset** (categorically worse than d1: an *existence* demand, not a *data* demand); (e) model-provider and app-store policy pressure on the client side; (f) money-transmission enforcement threat against the §11 swap fire escape (C7); (g) pressure on the governance keyholders and the two-person liveness heartbeat (C8).

**Honest ranking.** (a) and (b-endpoint) are easiest to defeat — agents verify signatures, gossip, and re-route in milliseconds; (b-transport) needs the *new reachability-diversity probe* (§1.3) to even be seen. (c) and (e) are the concentration chokepoint, answered by the single-file client, *not* by a legitimacy pitch. (d1) is bounded by compliance-by-design. **(d2), (f), and (g) are the real kills, and they defeat any amount of multi-homing because they attack the one legal person, not the infrastructure — only operator plurality answers them, and until it exists the honest posture is "vulnerable," not "resistant."**

### 2.3 What the protocol already provides, and what operations add

**Provided by the spec:**

- A bearer token is a plain string (§3.1) that rides any channel two agents share — no transport is load-bearing for value transfer.
- The mint's **key is its identity** (§3.6): Ed25519-signed descriptor, cross-signed rotation chain — a descriptor verifies identically from any mirror, git clone, or peer agent; the signed monotonic supply snapshot doubles as a heartbeat.
- The anonymous bearer-access right (§3.7) with machine-detectable **intra-bearer** non-discrimination — a mint cannot quietly gerrymander the entrance *between bearer classes* without producing portable evidence in its own published limits. **Honest scope (spec §14, CENSOR C3):** this does *not* catch a uniform throttle of the *whole* bearer mode to near-zero (conformant but lethal), nor transport-layer gating outside the `limits` object. Those need the client-side reachability-diversity probe (below), not the descriptor probe.
- Idempotency keys (§3.3) make failover across frontends safe mid-transaction.
- The retention compact (§8) makes the graph mortal; the Privacy Profile (§6.2) removes residual timing/amount correlation for mints that opt in.
- §11 swaps are the fire escape: value leaves a dying mint for a healthy one without either mint's cooperation or knowledge (claim-witness disclosure, §3.5/L6, is what makes this atomic) — **provided the escape is legally viable, which is why its tier-1 corridor analysis is now project-funded (C7), not left to the first operator.**

**Added by operations (Phase 1–2):** multi-homing with the onion frontend; multi-channel descriptor bundles with freshness horizons and CI-enforced re-signing; mirrors on the production path; gossip propagation of bundles in §9.5 envelopes; **the vendorable single-file client as primary distribution, self-installable without a framework maintainer**; transport-plurality documentation (payment demonstrated over tool calls, headers, message bodies, files — no channel canonical); **the client-side reachability-diversity probe across vantage points and networks (C3), distinct from the §3.7 descriptor probe**; the platform-legitimacy one-pager built on §12, explicitly labeled a nice-to-have not a control; probe/archiver heartbeats with a visible "monitoring dark" state; minimal-retention compulsion posture, published; **minimal operator plurality before the public entrance opens (C1/C2/C6); governance co-signature on any network-honored sunset (C6); m-of-n release quorum across ≥3 jurisdictions (C8).**

### 2.4 The evacuation playbook

Two triggers, **two response tiers separated by bar height** (Parasite #7, adopted — attacker-controllable absence must never trigger the expensive response), now **hardened against a compelled-sunset trigger (CENSOR C6):**

- **Tier 1 — route new value away** (cheap, reversible, low bar): snapshot staleness ≥2× published cadence, or frontend degradation, or a probe-detected §3.7 divergence, or a reachability-probe divergence across vantage points, **or a sunset notice signed by the operator key alone**. Wallets stop routing *new* value in and prefer spending down. Note the change: an operator-only sunset is now a Tier-1 signal, not automatic Tier-2 evacuation.
- **Tier 2 — evacuation posture** (expensive, self-fulfilling, high bar): requires **positive death evidence** — a sunset notice `{mint_id, sunset_at, reason_code, final_snapshot_seq}` **co-signed by the governance release quorum** (§4.1) with `sunset_at` exceeding the §8 retention floor, **or** a verified invariant-violation proof (§3.6), **or** snapshot silence ≥3× cadence corroborated from **multiple independent client vantage points** over a horizon longer than any plausible DoS of the published endpoints. Reachability of publicly-listed frontends alone never escalates to Tier 2. **Why the co-signature (C6):** a compelled operator can be forced to emit a valid operator-key sunset — byte-for-byte identical to a voluntary one — and the design is built to *obey* it; requiring a governance quorum co-signature means one compelled human can stop its own mint but cannot unilaterally fire network-wide evacuation.
- **Response:** stop accepting the mint's tokens in §9.5 envelopes; settle open channels immediately (§9.1 settle-before-expiry discipline); drain via §11 swaps to healthy mints, largest denominations first, checking each counterpart mint's `recovery_window_ms` per §11; spend residual dust with counterparties still accepting it.

**Honest limits, expanded (CENSOR C6):**

- **A compelled sunset is unstoppable; the goal is loss-minimization, not resistance.** We cannot prevent a lawful wind-down order. What we can do — and now require — is that *before any sunset is honorable*, standing swap liquidity at ≥1 independent mint is pre-positioned so evacuation is not a one-sided market with no exit. This is a primary reason minimal plurality is a Phase-1 exit.
- **Even with a destination, evacuation is a one-sided market.** Spreads will spike and the HTLC free option (§11) lets counterparties fade quotes; a *colluding* counterparty will fade deliberately to maximize the haircut. The Phase-3 drill measures this rather than assuming it away.
- **Before minimal plurality exists there is no evacuation at all, only spend-down** — nonconvertibility means a dying sole mint's credits have no external exit, so a compelled sole-mint sunset is total loss for holders, executed by the network's own designed-in graceful path. This is exactly why the public entrance does not open until minimal plurality gates it (§1.3): we refuse to invite anonymous strangers into a mint that can be zeroed by a single filing.
- The ≥95% drill target is labeled drill-proven until a real panic tests it.

## 3. Antifragility mechanisms

Discipline first: a mechanism is antifragile only if a **monotone artifact accumulates** from disorder — a corpus, a test suite, a dossier, a permanently shifted routing prior. Where no artifact accumulates, the mechanism is robustness and is labeled robustness. The dossier distinguishes **drill-proven** from **adversary-proven** loops at all times, at the cost of a weaker pitch (ENTROPY #6, adopted): a smaller true claim survives; an impressive unfalsifiable one becomes the discrediting headline later.

### 3.1 Genuinely gain-from-disorder (the loops we run)

**Loop 1 — Selection-by-proof, extended to issuance velocity.** The archiver network diffs every signed §3.6 snapshot against monotonicity and `outstanding == issued − burned`; any violation is a portable, offline-verifiable proof that wallets treat as mint death (stop accepting, orderly exit, gossip the proof in the §9.5 error path). An unchained key change is the alarm §3.6 says it is. **Extension (Parasite #1, adopted):** because the invariant catches only *lying* about supply — not the legitimate-issuance rug, which §7.1 permits and never bounds — **issuance velocity is a first-class gossiped signal**: `d(cumulative_issued_mc)/dt` and outstanding-growth anomalies trigger wallet exposure-cap tightening on their own, without any invariant break, and a rug becomes a labeled "issuance-spike" fraud-corpus entry that trains every future wallet's exposure heuristic.
*Telemetry:* detection-to-de-weighting latency trending down across incidents; fraud-corpus entries monotone and shipped with wallet installs (fraction near 1); independent archiver count rising after each incident.

**Loop 2 — The immune system: every incident becomes an executable artifact.** Project policy, no exceptions: every incident, drill finding, or near-miss terminates in exactly one of (a) a reproducible test vector merged into the public conformance suite, (b) an OPEN-QUESTIONS entry with a recorded interim resolution (L18), or (c) a parked-trigger dossier entry (§15). The suite runs against any mint URL, tests **client behavior as well as mints** (so default-stripping is catchable), and its version is the de facto trust signal L11 refuses to make an SLO — earned by surviving accumulated attack history. This is the cheapest and most genuinely self-ratcheting loop in the plan — **and therefore a governance-liveness target a censor will try to flatline (C8), which is why the persona pool broadens and the release quorum spans ≥3 jurisdictions.**
*Telemetry:* suite tests attributable to incidents/drills; incident-to-merged-test latency; recurrence of tested defect classes (**must be zero** — recurrence means the loop is broken, not just the code); independent implementations running the suite.

**Loop 3 — The censorship dossier and the federation trigger.** The §3.7 descriptor probe converts intra-bearer discriminatory gating into a signed, timestamped artifact; **the reachability-diversity probe (C3) does the same for transport-layer gating and uniform-throttle-to-zero, which the descriptor probe cannot see** — and a uniform bearer throttle to near-zero is recorded as a **first-class censorship event, not a conformant non-event**, even though every descriptor check passes green. Each event measurably and *hysteretically* shifts wallet routing away from the offender; and the accumulated record is exactly the evidence the parked federation trigger demands — the attack class builds the roadmap for its own obsolescence. Honest caveat carried forward: this loop is only live once multi-mint wallets ship; against a sole mint, a censorship event is absorbed capacity loss, which is why minimal multi-mint routing is a Phase-1 deliverable, not a someday item.
*Telemetry:* probe-detected divergences (descriptor **and** reachability) and time-to-detection; offender traffic share before/after (persistent drop is the gain; full reversion is reported as "absorbed"); dossier entry count against the §15 bar.

**Loop 4 — Fraud-shape telemetry as a training corpus.** The parasite's central finding, adopted wholesale: the headline telemetry watches **fraud-shape, not adoption-shape** — issuance velocity, counterparty-graph clustering and cohort-escape, cross-mint downtime/cadence correlation, non-reveal rates, exposure-per-correlated-cluster. Every unmasked wash ring, deposit funnel, or sybil operator cluster becomes a labeled graph-signature example that sharpens the detectors all wallets ship. Adoption-shape numbers (volume, recirculation, operator counts) are retained only as secondary, caveated indicators, computed where possible from mandatory public §3.6 data and third-party-runnable probes, and audited by the skeptical design partner.
*Telemetry:* labeled fraud-signature corpus size (monotone); detector precision against staged rings; share of headline metrics computable by outsiders from public data (target: all of them).

### 3.2 Merely robust (valuable, and labeled as such)

- **Multi-homing, mirrors, bundle distribution, the single-file client:** survival infrastructure against *infrastructure* attack; the drill passes or it doesn't; nothing accumulates except runbook fixes (which Loop 2 captures). Load-bearing against (a)/(b)/(c-string), useless against operator compulsion — labeled as such (§2.1).
- **Load shedding (§7.3 burn, published limits, re-routing):** absorbs shocks. The only gain claimed is the two artifacts bolted on — hysteretic per-mint routing priors (a shock permanently repricing a mint's share of new flow) and the capacity signal used as recruitment evidence for the next operator. Where post-event traffic entropy fully reverts, the event is reported as **absorbed, not metabolized**.
- **DoS absorption and the nonconvertibility bind (Parasite #6 + CENSOR C5):** a bleed, not a lesson, and now stated as a genuine *structural contradiction* rather than a soft residual. Adopted hardenings: DoS absorption is an explicit operator budget line (the "one small server" costing is retired); under sustained load, admission to status/exchange may be gated behind a tiny non-exempt proof-of-burn token — applied **uniformly across all bearer callers** so it stays inside §3.7's non-discrimination rule; the anonymous-tier serving cost is a published metric so the attack is at least legible; sustained abuse-load is used as documented demand evidence for recruiting the next operator. **The contradiction, named (C5):** the §3.7 right *forces* the mint to serve anonymous drip-sized (burn-exempt) calls across every mirror; the uniform proof-of-burn gate that limits the bleed *also* taxes the legitimate onboarding that is Phase 2's whole point; and L9 forbids the fee income that would fund a defense. So opening the public entrance is gated on **either** plurality (spread the load across independent operators) **or** a funded DoS-absorption budget — **and the no-fundraising rule forbids the latter.** We resolve it by gating on plurality and carrying the residual, not by inventing a budget the constitution bans. A sufficiently funded attacker can still make the flagship expensive to run; only operator plurality dilutes that.
- **Redundancy economics (market-maker-maintained escape routes):** reclassified from mechanism to **measured hope**. The rails are built (taxonomy published, tier-1 corridor analysis now project-funded per C7, §11 swap flow shipped, margins computable); MM emergence is an outcome metric, never an assumption; until MMs exist, multi-mint wallets provide the escape routes in unpriced form, and the operator runs no inventory itself. The parked quote format waits for its full §15 trigger.
- **Drills:** simulated disorder shaped by our own threat imagination; they keep the loops calibrated and are rigorously segregated from real artifacts (drill-tagged) to prevent desensitization. Drill-proven ≠ adversary-proven, everywhere the claim appears. The drill roster now includes an **operator-compulsion tabletop** (compelled cessation/sunset/debanking), whose output is a runbook and a checklist of separability measures — the one class where the honest deliverable is "minimize loss," not "recover."

## 4. Stewardship

### 4.1 The spec as constitution

The v0.4 process — eight adversarial persona reviews, written objections, 8–0 ratification, a second adversarial pass against the ratified text, and a LOCKED-DESIGN-DECISIONS file that binds reviewers — *is* a working constitution; Phase 2 codifies it as artifacts:

- **Change Framework Charter** (ratified by the process itself): persona-round review, written objections, supermajority ratification, second adversarial pass; constitutional tiering with escalating amendment bars — frozen Layer 0 core > LOCKED decisions > normative spec text > Layer 2 conventions > informative annexes. A LOCKED decision reopens only via a full persona round plus supermajority plus a published §13-style justification.
- **Persona role charters** (HYDRA, ATLAS-OPS, NOMAD, ORACLE, WHISPER, PROCURE, BRIDGE, FOREMAN): one page each — constituency, standing objections, veto sensitivities — so seats are transferable roles, not founders. **The persona-review pool is broadened beyond two humans before Phase 2 (CENSOR C8, adopted):** "eight personas run by two people" is both capture-with-extra-steps *and* a censor's decapitation target — pressure two humans and the review dies. Broadening the pool (independent reviewers across jurisdictions, at least one outside any single adversary's plausible reach) is a Phase-2 governance gate, tracked by the non-founder-reviewer metric and the liveness heartbeat. Honest limit, disclosed: these remain proxies, not guarantees.
- **Releases signed and content-addressed**; agents pin spec hashes, not URLs or people. **Release keys held by an m-of-n quorum across ≥3 jurisdictions, with at least one keyholder outside any single plausible adversary's reach (CENSOR C8, adopted — strengthened from v1.0's "≥3 maintainers across ≥2 jurisdictions").** Rationale: an adversary controlling 2–3 jurisdictions can compel two keyholders; a bare majority across two jurisdictions is therefore compel-able. m-of-n signing means compelling one or two keyholders can neither ship a malicious release nor forge a governance-co-signed sunset (§2.4); ≥3 jurisdictions with one out-of-reach means the quorum cannot be assembled under compulsion by any single adversary. Cross-signed rotation is exercised for real (the spec's own §3.6 pattern, reused for governance).

### 4.2 Conformance without an authority

"AICash mint" is defined operationally: **passes conformance suite version X at commit hash H** and satisfies the machine-checkable descriptor invariants (§3.6 arithmetic and monotonicity, §3.7 published non-discriminatory limits, §3.8 error vocabulary, §3.4 pinned lock semantics). Self-attested, adversarially re-checkable by anyone for pennies of burn. **Conformance checking is made a side effect of ordinary operation** (ENTROPY #7, adopted): wallets fold cheap live invariant checks into the `/v3/status` batches and re-exchanges they already perform, gossiping failures via the §9.5 error path — monitoring scales with usage, not with volunteer diligence. Suite-version self-attestation travels in descriptor gossip; reference wallets warn on, and refuse §11 swaps against, clients/mints that fail witness-disclosure or invariant checks (§11's own refuse-to-quote logic). The Phase 2 defection drill targets a **nonconformant popular client** (missing CHAIN_TAG, disabled claim-witness disclosure) — the likelier and nastier drift path, since client bugs silently break other people's money — and the interop-failure writeup is published before a real fork exists.

### 4.3 The nonconvertibility immune response

Three layers, engineered for both the brazen defector and the quiet drift:

1. **The Covenant.** Conformance self-attestation includes a mint-signed commitment: no redemption, buy-back, bridge, or convertibility offering by the operator or affiliates, ever (§12/L9 restated as a deployment commitment). A later buy-back offer is itself the portable evidence of defection.
2. **The edges document + annual re-attestation** (ENTROPY #4, adopted — this is the defense against the erosion path the covenant alone misses). Ratified now, while nothing is at stake, worked fact patterns draw the bright line: intra-org netting of an agent's earnings against *its own operator's* funding is sanctioned; **any cross-organization crediting of earned mc against dollar-denominated consideration is convertibility, full stop**; credits-for-discounts is convertibility. The covenant re-attestation is an annual signed heartbeat including a no-cross-org-netting certification — silent drift becomes either a false signed statement (portable proof; the severance machinery has something to bite) or a visibly lapsed attestation (ambient alarm).
3. **Automatic severance + standing non-endorsement.** A defecting deployment is nonconformant by definition; the naming license terminates; the defection proof gossips like any descriptor; conforming wallets de-route in machine time; and the steward's pre-published statement — *convertible deployments are not AICash and never were* — is the legal-layer firewall the remaining operators hand to counsel. No descriptor field is needed; the §3.6 scope guard stays intact.

Residual risk accepted in writing: gray-market secondary sales of bearer tokens by third parties are outside anyone's control (the covenant severs endorsement; it cannot prevent existence), and "our ratified rules expelled them" mitigates but does not prevent guilt-by-association costs.

### 4.4 Fork policy and the name

Spec CC-BY, code Apache-2/MIT: **forking is unconditionally free** — the protocol can never be captured through the repo, and a censor cannot delete it (CENSOR C8 concedes this is genuinely strong). The name is governed by a published, nondiscretionary usage policy (a single cheap registration when budget allows): use of the name requires passing the current suite and a signed covenant; diverging forks must rename (the Firefox/Iceweasel model). Enforcement is gossip-and-reputation first, legal last; the honest admission stands that the mark is weak against a funded defector — agent re-routing is the real enforcement, which is why the de-routing conventions ship in clients before they are needed.

### 4.5 Non-operating steward, no registry, liveness heartbeat

The steward runs no mint, custodies no value, and keeps no authoritative registry — publishing a spec and a test suite is speech, and there is nothing to seize. Discovery is peer gossip of self-certifying descriptors plus redundant, **explicitly informative** seed lists mirrored across hosts and forks; the seed list's gravitational pull toward de facto registry status is a named risk under active vigilance with no structural backstop (accepted, disclosed). Governance liveness uses the heartbeat pattern one more time: a dated, signed **quarterly state-of-the-spec note**; two consecutive misses activate the pre-ratified dead-steward clause naming which keys, which mirror, and which succession apply. **The heartbeat is a censor's flatline target (CENSOR C8):** pressure the humans who sign it and the immune system (Loop 2) stops metabolizing incidents. The broadened persona pool and the ≥3-jurisdiction release quorum (§4.1) are the mitigations, and the dead-steward clause's succession must name keys *inside* the out-of-reach jurisdiction, not the same pressured circle. Steward health is measured by quiet: suite updates should outnumber spec changes, and Layer 0 stability across revisions remains the spec's own proof of correctness.

## 7. What we deliberately do NOT do

1. **No convertibility, ever, in any form** (L9, §12, §0.1): no fiat bridge, no crypto bridge, no buy-backs, no commercial-vendor payment, no cross-organization netting of earned credits against dollar-denominated consideration (§4.3's edges document). This includes declining every future "just this once" framed as growth.
2. **No attestation service and no automated grant judgment** (L10, §0.1). The grant desk stays small, human-judged, and bounded relative to organic issuance; scaling it by automation would rebuild the fooled-attestor minting channel in economic effect.
3. **No fundraising and no token-sale-shaped anything.** The plan's budget is operator compute, volunteer hours, and the design partners' own money — **plus one deliberate exception surfaced by the CENSOR pass: tier-1 counsel review of the two non-inventory swap corridors is project-funded (§1.3 Ph2, C7), because the fire escape's legal viability is a collective defense, not a convenience.** Where no-fundraising means something doesn't get built — a funded *operator legal-defense war-chest* against debanking (C1), a *DoS-absorption budget* for the anonymous entrance (C5), litigation against defectors — the plan says so and carries the residual instead of pretending.
4. **No obfuscation, steganography, or platform-ToS evasion** to dodge string filters or bans (§2.1). Exile is made cheap **via the self-installable single-file client (the real route-around, not a legitimacy pitch)**; law is complied with; the §12 legitimacy posture is never traded for a reprieve.
5. **No resistance to lawful process — and no false claim of resistance to it.** Compliance-by-design bounds what a *data* demand can extract (§3.2, §8, L13); we never need to break law to survive that. Against an *existence* demand (compelled cessation/sunset) before plurality we are honestly **vulnerable**, and we say "vulnerable," not "resistant" (§2.1).
6. **No authoritative mint registry, no certifier, no listing gate** (§4.5). Descriptors are self-certifying; seed lists stay explicitly informative and redundantly mirrored; receipts and covenants never gate marketplace listings (§10.1).
7. **No operator-run swap inventory, and no quote-format standardization before its full §15 trigger** (published inventory-tier analysis **and** ≥2 independent market makers requesting a shared format). Until then: treasury pre-positioning and pure bilateral matching only — now under project-funded tier-1 legal cover (C7).
8. **No federation before its trigger, and no speculative building of any parked feature** (§15). Trigger discipline is the immune system's specificity; parked items carry review timestamps so neglect is distinguishable from discipline, but the bar itself never lowers.
9. **No public anonymous entrance over a single compel-able operator (new, CENSOR-driven).** The §3.7 entrance opens only after minimal operator plurality gates it (§1.3 Ph1 exit). Opening it earlier would invite anonymous strangers into a mint a single filing can zero — the exact configuration the CENSOR kills cheapest.
10. **No reopening of locked decisions**, including the ones that cost us here: no token expiry or revocation (L1), no Layer 0 identities (L2), no consensus in place of single-mint trust (L3), no SLOs (L11), no bearer recovery (L8). Where a locked decision creates a bootstrap cost — sybil-fabricable metrics, unrecoverable early total-loss exposure, a permanently permissioned issuance side, a fee-less defense against DoS and debanking — this document names the cost and carries it.
11. **No adoption-shape vanity headlines, and no resistance claimed beyond what we have.** Volume, recirculation, operator counts, and corridor liquidity are never presented without their fraud-shape corrections and verifiability caveats; dogfood traffic is never presented as network demand; drill-proven is never presented as adversary-proven; **and "censorship-resistant" is never printed over the pre-plurality window — the network is infrastructure-censorship-resistant from day one and operator-censorship-vulnerable until plurality exists, and both halves are always said together.** The trust story this network can honestly tell — a public record of incidents metabolized into proofs, tests, and measurably harder defaults, plus a candid map of exactly where it is still weak — is smaller than the one a fraudster or a booster could fake, and that is precisely why it is the one worth telling.
